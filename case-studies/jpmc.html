Problem

Customers need immediate access to funds from deposited checks, but existing solutions are inconvenient, expensive, and lack fee transparency.

These customers often face financial stress and need immediate access for rent and essentials. Today, they turn to third-party cashers or retailers like Walmart, where limited access and high fees deepen that strain.

Insight

Customers don't just need faster access. They need control over how much they access, clarity on what it costs upfront, and reassurance they won't overdraft.

Business goals

  • Increase adoption and revenue from instant-access features
  • Reduce operational costs by shifting check cashing from branches to digital
  • Establish Chase as a leader in instant fund access among traditional banks

User goals

  • Know the fee upfront before committing
  • Access only what they need, not the full amount
  • Feel confident that accessing funds won't risk overdrafting their account

Design opportunity

How might we deliver an instant-access experience that gives customers transparency and control over their funds, while balancing risk and business needs?

Solution

Chase Instant Access makes mobile check deposits available instantly for a 2% fee.

Get full or partial funds, on your terms.

Upfront fee and net amount, no surprises.

Instant access on your schedule, not business hours.

Impact & Validation

While the feature is slated for a future release, we validated its direction through 3 rounds of usability testing, including a focused overdraft edge case.

Phase 1: Baseline viability — standard Quick Deposit flow

Instant Access was broadly understood, perceived as optional, and non-alarming for general customers. This validated the feature itself, but left the overdraft edge case untested.

Moderated testing with 9 participants

Phase 2: Overdraft stress test — five messaging strategies

Recruited recently-overdrafted customers. Tested no messaging, speedbumps, legal-approved copy, upstream warnings, and confirmation-page messaging in realistic scenarios.

Unmoderated testing with 9 participants

Phase 3: Large scale overdraft stress test

Scaled the overdraft scenario with a larger participant pool to pressure-test comprehension, trust, and timing across the confirmation experience.

Unmoderated testing with 120 participants

Most participants struggled to read and fully comprehend the overdraft messaging in context, which led directly to the first major decision.

Key Decision 1

Not allow this feature to be enabled for customers with overdraft fees, as the gaps in our knowledge could not be properly solved by over communication.

Key Decision 2

Place the offer on the confirmation page instead of earlier in the flow.

Key Decision 3

Favor concise, well-timed messaging over adding more warnings upstream in the journey.

Reflection

What I've learned

Balancing cross-functional tradeoffs

Navigated competing team needs by making intentional design adjustments, like condensing the confirmation layout to surface instant access, without disrupting the core experience.

Designing for trust under pressure

When customers are already under financial stress, timing and clarity matter as much as the feature itself. Better sequencing often beats more messaging.
